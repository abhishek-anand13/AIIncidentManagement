# SOP
Critical Incident Response

# Purpose
This SOP defines the required response process for critical incidents that threaten major service outage, widespread impact, or significant business disruption.

# Scope
This SOP applies when multiple services are unavailable or severely degraded, when a large number of users are affected, or when the incident requires executive or cross-functional escalation.

# Responsibilities
- Incident Manager: declares the incident, coordinates response activities, and ensures executive communication
- Operations Team: executes mitigation steps, validates service recovery, and monitors stability
- Platform Team: supports infrastructure recovery, routing, capacity, and resiliency actions
- Engineering Team: investigates the root cause, implements fixes, and verifies remediation
- Management: provides oversight, approves business-impact decisions, and supports prioritization

# Procedure
1. Declare the incident as critical and open a coordinated response bridge
2. Assign an incident manager and confirm the incident scope and impact
3. Prioritize restoration of customer-facing services and critical business workflows
4. Activate the relevant runbooks and known-issue references for mitigation
5. Maintain regular status updates using a structured communication cadence
6. Document mitigation actions, observed risks, and recovery progress in the incident record
7. Confirm service restoration, validate monitoring health, and close the incident formally

# Escalation Matrix
- Severity Critical: escalate immediately to engineering leadership, operations leadership, and business stakeholders
- Severity High: escalate to the incident manager and service owners for cross-team coordination
- Severity Medium: escalate when impact expands, recovery exceeds expected time, or business risk increases

# Communication Guidelines
Incident updates should be factual, concise, and time-based. Each update should include the current status, impact, actions underway, known risks, and the next planned update time.

# Best Practices
- Preserve a single source of truth for incident status and actions
- Use runbooks and known issues to reduce time to resolution
- Keep stakeholders informed without speculation or unsupported claims
- Capture lessons learned immediately after recovery to improve future response
